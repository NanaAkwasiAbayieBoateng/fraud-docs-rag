# Money Mules

## What a money mule is

A money mule is someone who receives stolen or fraudulently obtained
money into their own bank account and moves it onward — by transfer,
withdrawal, or cryptocurrency purchase — usually keeping a small cut.
Mules give criminals a layer of distance from the original crime: the
money that reaches the criminal's own accounts appears to come from an
ordinary customer, which makes tracing and recovery harder. Acting as a
mule is money laundering, even if the person claims they did not know
where the money came from.

## How mules are recruited

Recruiters advertise on social media, messaging apps, and job boards.
The offers look like easy work: "payment processor," "transfer agent,"
"crypto trader," or work-from-home administration paying a commission
per transaction. Some recruits are students or people in debt who are
promised fast cash for doing almost nothing. Others are drawn in through
romance contacts or fake employers who first build trust, then ask for
a "favour." A newer pattern asks recruits to open accounts in their own
name and hand over the cards, PINs, and online banking details entirely.

## Red flags of a mule recruitment offer

Warning signs include a job that was never interviewed for, an employer
who communicates only by chat or encrypted message, being asked to use
a personal bank account for company money, payment in commission for
simply receiving and forwarding funds, and instructions to lie to bank
staff about what the money is for. Any offer where the main duty is
moving other people's money through your account is laundering, however
it is dressed up.

## Consequences for mules

Mules face account closure, a fraud marker that can leave them unable
to open bank accounts, get credit, or hold some jobs for years, and
criminal prosecution for money laundering, which carries prison
sentences in most jurisdictions. Ignorance is rarely a defence once the
red flags are pointed out. Banks that spot mule behaviour freeze the
account and report it, and the funds are returned to victims where
possible. Anyone who has been approached should stop, keep the
messages, and report the recruiter to their bank and the police.
