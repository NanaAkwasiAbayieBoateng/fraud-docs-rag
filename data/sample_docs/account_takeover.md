# Account Takeover

## What account takeover is

Account takeover (ATO) is when a criminal gains control of a victim's
existing account — email, banking, shopping, or social media — and uses
it as the victim. Attackers get in with passwords harvested from data
breaches (reused credentials are tried automatically in credential
stuffing attacks), with phishing, or with malware that steals session
cookies. Once inside, they typically change the password, email address,
and phone number on the account so the real owner is locked out and
recovery messages go to the criminal instead.

## Warning signs of a takeover

Signs include password-reset or login alerts you did not request,
multi-factor prompts arriving when you are not signing in, unfamiliar
devices in the account's security settings, changed contact details,
and orders, transfers, or messages you do not recognise. A sudden
inability to log in with a password you are sure is right is itself a
strong signal: the attacker may already have changed it.

## How to recover and prevent takeover

Act through the provider's official app or website — never links from
an alert message. Reset the password to something unique, sign out all
sessions, review and restore the contact details and multi-factor
methods on the account, and check connected accounts that share the
email address, starting with the email account itself. To prevent
takeover, use a unique password per account (a password manager makes
this practical), keep multi-factor authentication on, and treat
unexpected approval prompts as a stop sign, not a nuisance to dismiss.
