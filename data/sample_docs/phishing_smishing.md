# Phishing and Smishing

## What phishing and smishing are

Phishing is a fraudulent message — usually an email — that impersonates
a trusted organisation to trick the recipient into revealing credentials,
card details, or one-time passcodes, or into installing malware.
Smishing is the same technique delivered by text message (SMS). The
messages create urgency: an account is "about to be closed," a delivery
is "held," or a payment "failed." Links lead to lookalike websites that
harvest whatever the victim types; phone numbers in the message lead to
the criminals, not the real organisation.

## How to spot a phishing message

Warning signs include a sender address that almost — but not quite —
matches the real organisation's domain, generic greetings instead of
your name, spelling and grammar slips, and links whose true destination
differs from the text displayed. Legitimate banks do not ask customers
to move money, share a one-time passcode, or install screen-sharing
software. A message that pressures you to act immediately, before you
can think or check, is using the single most reliable marker of a scam.

## What to do if you responded

If you entered credentials on a fake site, change the password from a
clean device and turn on multi-factor authentication. If you shared card
details, contact the bank using the number on the back of the card so
the card can be blocked and reissued. Report the message to the
impersonated organisation and your national phishing reporting service,
then delete it. Never use the links or phone numbers in the suspicious
message itself to make that contact.
