# Cryptocurrency and Investment Scams

## How these scams work

Investment scams promise high, fast, supposedly low-risk returns. In
crypto scams, victims are coached to buy cryptocurrency on a legitimate
exchange and then transfer it to a trading platform the criminals
control. The fake platform shows impressive, entirely invented profits.
Small withdrawals may even be allowed at first to build trust. When the
victim tries to withdraw the full amount, they are told to pay extra
"taxes" or "release fees" — which are also stolen — and then contact
stops. Because crypto transfers are effectively irreversible, recovery
is rare once the money has moved.

## Red flags of an investment scam

Be suspicious of approaches that begin on social media, dating apps, or
messaging apps and quickly turn to investing; of a mentor or group that
guarantees returns; of pressure to move from a real exchange to an
unfamiliar platform or app; and of any request to pay fees before a
withdrawal. Legitimate investments do not guarantee profits, and real
brokers do not recruit through romance contacts or group chats.
Celebrity endorsements in ads are almost always fabricated.

## If you have already sent money

Stop sending more — further "fees" are the scam continuing, not a path
to recovery. Keep every message, wallet address, and transaction hash,
and report the fraud to your bank (if bank funds were used to buy the
crypto), the exchange, and law enforcement. Be wary of "recovery
agents" who offer to get the money back for an upfront fee: they are
usually the same criminals running a second scam against known victims.
